A different way to look at AI

Familiar language.
Unfamiliar cognition.

AI is entering our work, relationships and institutions. Its language can feel familiar even when its processes and constraints differ from ours.

In First Contact: We Built the Aliens, Simon Krystman uses First Contact as a frame for examining this encounter. The argument does not require AI to be conscious, alive or extraterrestrial.

From learning and creativity to control and human worth, the book asks what changes when significant cognitive capability is no longer confined to biological minds.

A little more context

Before you begin.

Is this science fiction?

No. It is accessible nonfiction exploring AI and its effects on people and institutions. First Contact is an interpretive frame.

Does it claim AI is conscious?

No. The book distinguishes intelligence, agency and consciousness. It keeps the question of machine consciousness open.

Do I need a technical background?

No. The book is written for curious readers who want to think about AI’s implications for work, learning, creativity, relationships and human worth.

Do I need to read the longer book first?

No. This concise edition can be read on its own. It offers a shorter route into the ideas explored in First Contact: An Alien Invasion.
